## **PART 3: BEYOND EMAIL - MULTI-CHANNEL PHISHING (20 Slides)**

**Slide 1: Phishing Beyond the Inbox**
* Phishing has evolved beyond email
* Attackers use any communication channel available
* Same psychological principles, different delivery methods

**Slide 2: Smishing (SMS Phishing)**
* **Definition:** Phishing via text messages
* **Common Examples:**
  - "Your package delivery failed - track here"
  - "Your bank account is locked - verify now"
  - "You've won a prize - claim here"
* **Volume:** 87% increase in smishing attacks in 2024

**Slide 3: Smishing Red Flags**
* Unknown sender numbers
* Shortened links (bit.ly, tinyurl)
* Misspellings or informal tone
* Urgent instructions
* Requests for personal information
* Prize notifications you didn't enter

**Slide 4: Smishing Defense Strategies**
* **Don't Click:** Avoid links in unexpected texts
* **Don't Reply:** Don't engage or provide information
* **Verify:** Contact organization through official channels
* **Report:** Forward to IT/Security (screenshot + sender ID)
* **Delete:** Remove after reporting

**Slide 5: Vishing (Voice Phishing)**
* **Definition:** Phone-based social engineering
* **Common Scenarios:**
  - "We detected suspicious activity on your account"
  - "I'm from IT and need your login to update systems"
  - "This is HR verifying your employment details"

**Slide 6: Vishing Red Flags**
* Caller requesting passwords or OTPs
* Pressure to act immediately
* Refusal to call back on official numbers
* Requests for remote computer access
* Claims of urgent security threats

**Slide 7: Vishing Defense Protocol**
* **Never Share:** Passwords, OTPs, or remote access
* **Hang Up:** End suspicious calls immediately
* **Call Back:** Use official numbers from company directory
* **Verify:** Use internal channels (Teams, Slack, email)
* **Report:** Note caller ID, time, details for security

**Slide 8: Social Media Phishing**
* **Platforms:** LinkedIn, Facebook, Twitter, Instagram
* **Methods:**
  - Fake job offers with malicious links
  - Impersonation of colleagues or executives
  - Fake connection requests
  - Malicious direct messages

**Slide 9: Social Media Red Flags**
* Connection requests from unknown people
* Messages with urgent requests or links
* Job offers that seem too good to be true
* Profiles with limited history or connections
* Requests to move conversations to personal email

**Slide 10: Social Media Defense**
* **Privacy Settings:** Limit public information
* **Connection Vetting:** Verify before accepting
* **Link Caution:** Don't click links in messages
* **Profile Verification:** Check employment details
* **Reporting:** Report suspicious profiles to platform and security

**Slide 11: QR Code Phishing (Quishing)**
* **Definition:** Malicious QR codes
* **Locations:**
  - Office posters and flyers
  - Cafes and public spaces
  - Email attachments
  - Product packaging
* **Growth:** 300% increase in quishing attacks

**Slide 12: Quishing Defense**
* **Scan Cautiously:** Use company-approved QR scanners
* **URL Preview:** Check destination before proceeding
* **Source Verification:** Only scan codes from trusted sources
* **Alternative Access:** Use direct URLs when possible
* **Reporting:** Report suspicious QR codes

**Slide 13: Messaging App Phishing**
* **Platforms:** WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack, Teams
* **Methods:**
  - Impersonation of colleagues
  - Fake urgent requests
  - Malicious file sharing
  - Fake group invitations

**Slide 14: Messaging App Red Flags**
* Contacts behaving unusually
* Urgent financial or data requests
* Links to unfamiliar websites
* Requests to bypass normal procedures
* Messages outside normal hours

**Slide 15: Collaboration Tool Security**
* **Verify Identity:** Confirm unusual requests
* **Link Safety:** Hover over links before clicking
* **File Scanning:** Be cautious with unexpected files
* **Channel Verification:** Use appropriate channels for sensitive topics
* **Policy Awareness:** Follow company collaboration guidelines

**Slide 16: Deepfake & AI-Powered Phishing**
* **Technology:** AI-generated voice and video
* **Examples:**
  - Fake CEO video authorizing payments
  - AI voice mimicking executives on calls
  - Personalized phishing at scale
* **Risks:** Extreme realism increases success rates

**Slide 17: AI Phishing Defense**
* **Process Over Pressure:** Follow procedures regardless of urgency
* **Multi-Channel Verification:** Confirm through different communication methods
* **Pre-established Codes:** Use verification codes for sensitive requests
* **Awareness:** Understand AI capabilities and limitations
* **Reporting:** Report suspected deepfake attempts

**Slide 18: Physical Phishing Methods**
* **USB Drops:** Infected drives left in public areas
* **Fake Surveys:** Malicious QR codes or links
* **Shoulder Surfing:** Observing login credentials
* **Tailgating:** Following into secure areas
* **Fake IT Personnel:** Requesting physical access

**Slide 19: Physical Social Engineering Defense**
* **USB Policy:** Never use found drives
* **Access Control:** Challenge unknown individuals
* **Clean Desk:** Secure sensitive information
* **Awareness:** Question unexpected visitors
* **Reporting:** Report security breaches immediately

**Slide 20: Multi-Channel Defense Commitment**
* "I am vigilant across all communication channels"
* "I apply the same skepticism to texts and calls as email"
* "I verify unusual requests regardless of source"
* "I report suspicious activity in any form"
