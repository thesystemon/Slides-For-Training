## **PART 5: RESPONSE & REPORTING PROTOCOLS (20 Slides)**

**Slide 1: The Power of Rapid Response**
* "The fastest click can start an attack. The fastest report can stop it."
* Early reporting prevents company-wide incidents
* Your vigilance protects everyone

**Slide 2: Why Reporting Matters**
* **Stops Spread:** IT can block threat from reaching others
* **Reduces Damage:** Early detection prevents major incidents
* **Improves Defenses:** Every report trains security systems
* **Builds Culture:** Reporting becomes organizational habit
* **Fact:** Reporting within 5 minutes can prevent company-wide breach

**Slide 3: What to Report - Comprehensive List**
* **Phishing Emails:** Suspicious messages requesting information
* **Smishing:** Suspicious text messages with links
* **Vishing:** Suspicious phone calls requesting data
* **Social Media:** Fake profiles or suspicious messages
* **Physical Security:** Suspicious visitors or activities
* **Policy Violations:** Observed security procedure breaches

**Slide 4: Reporting Procedure - Step by Step**
1. **Identify:** Recognize suspicious communication
2. **Document:** Note details without interacting further
3. **Report:** Use official reporting channels
4. **Contain:** Don't forward or share the threat
5. **Follow-up:** Cooperate with investigation if needed

**Slide 5: Email Reporting Methods**
* **Preferred:** "Report Phishing" button in Outlook/Gmail
* **Alternative:** Forward as attachment to security@company.com
* **Subject Line:** "Suspected Phish - [Brief Description]"
* **Information:** Include full headers when possible
* **Confirmation:** Wait for acknowledgement when possible

**Slide 6: Non-Email Reporting**
* **Smishing:** Screenshot + forward to security team
* **Vishing:** Caller ID, time, details to security
* **Social Media:** Screenshot and profile information
* **Physical:** Description and photos if safe
* **In-Person:** Immediate supervisor and security notification

**Slide 7: Immediate Response - If You Clicked**
* **STOP:** Disconnect from network if possible
* **REPORT:** Call IT Help Desk immediately - don't use email
* **SECURE:** Password changes from clean device
* **SCAN:** Full system antivirus scan
* **DOCUMENT:** Details of what happened for investigation

**Slide 8: The "Clean-Up" Drill - Detailed Protocol**
* **DISCONNECT:** Unplug network cable or disable Wi-Fi
* **ALERT:** Call IT Help Desk directly: "I believe I've fallen for a phishing click"
* **SECURE:**
  - Change passwords from clean device
  - Device security scan required
  - Session revocation for affected accounts
* **REVIEW:** Work with manager and IT on remediation training

**Slide 9: Incident Documentation**
* **What:** Description of suspicious communication
* **When:** Date and time received
* **Who:** Sender information and contact details
* **How:** Method of communication and actions taken
* **Why:** Reasons for suspicion and potential impact

**Slide 10: Escalation Procedures**
* **Level 1:** Standard phishing attempt - report via normal channels
* **Level 2:** Credentials potentially compromised - immediate phone call
* **Level 3:** Financial or data impact - manager + security notification
* **Level 4:** Widespread or executive targeting - executive leadership alert
* **Level 5:** Active breach in progress - all-hands response activation

**Slide 11: What Happens After You Report**
* **SOC Analysis:** Experts examine headers, links, attachments
* **Containment:** Malicious elements blocked across organization
* **Eradication:** Compromised accounts secured and cleaned
* **Learning:** Incident improves defenses and training
* **Feedback:** Reporters may receive follow-up information

**Slide 12: No-Blame Reporting Culture**
* **Mistakes Happen:** Everyone can be tricked
* **Learning Opportunity:** Each incident improves security
* **Early Reporting:** Rewarded and celebrated
* **Continuous Improvement:** Focus on systems and processes
* **Psychological Safety:** Report without fear of punishment

**Slide 13: Reporting Champions Program**
* **Role Models:** Employees who exemplify good reporting
* **Recognition:** Public acknowledgement of vigilant reporters
* **Mentoring:** Experienced reporters help others
* **Feedback Loop:** Reporters help improve processes
* **Culture Building:** Creating security-first mindset

**Slide 14: Department-Specific Reporting**
* **Finance:** Payment and invoice verification procedures
* **HR:** Candidate and employee data protection
* **IT:** System access and credential requests
* **Executive:** High-value target special procedures
* **All:** General phishing and social engineering
