## **PART 7: CUSTOMER SUPPORT SECURITY (10 Slides)**

**Slide 1: Frontline Staff: First Defense Against Cyber Threats**
* "Your support team is the first human firewall"
* Access to sensitive information: customer PII, billing details, support logs
* Attackers exploit trust using social engineering

**Slide 2: Types of Social Engineering Attacks**
| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| Vishing (Voice) | Phone-based phishing | Fake customer requesting password/OTP |
| Smishing (SMS) | SMS-based phishing | Malicious link as billing alert |
| Phishing (Email) | Email impersonation | "Support ticket attached — click to resolve" |
| In-Person | Someone posing as IT/delivery | Requests office/system access |
| Chat/Helpdesk | Fake clients in chat platforms | Asking for internal data/credentials |

**Slide 3: Customer Data Handling Guidelines**
* **PII (Name, DOB, email, phone):** Confirm identity, avoid insecure channels
* **Financial Data:** Never provide full credit card numbers
* **Support Logs:** Limit access; mask sensitive data when sharing
* **Internal Notes:** Only share with team on need-to-know basis
* **Golden Rule:** Always verify request origin before sharing info

**Slide 4: Verification & Authentication**
* "Trust, but verify — always"
* Ask security questions without revealing answers
* Confirm via official email or phone number
* Never share credentials, passwords, or OTPs over phone/chat
* Escalate unusual or urgent requests to supervisor
* Use company-approved verification protocols

**Slide 5: Secure Ticketing & Chat Management**
* **Customer tickets:** Mask sensitive info in notes
* **Chat platforms:** Use official tools (Zendesk, Freshdesk, Intercom)
* **Email replies:** Avoid forwarding PII externally; use company links
* **Documentation:** Restrict access based on role
* **Attachment handling:** Scan for malware; use sandbox if uncertain

**Slide 6: Common Mistakes & Unsafe Practices**
* ⚠️ Sharing passwords to expedite support tickets
* ⚠️ Clicking unknown links sent by "customers"
* ⚠️ Giving account/billing info without verification
* ⚠️ Leaving workstation unlocked during calls
* ⚠️ Using personal devices/email for customer data
* ⚠️ Discussing sensitive cases in public areas

**Slide 7: Incident Reporting & Response**
1. **Identify:** Recognize suspicious request or data leak
2. **Report:** Inform IT/Security immediately with all details
3. **Contain:** Change passwords, revoke access, isolate systems
4. **Document:** Fill incident report with time, method, data involved
5. **Follow-up:** Participate in review and apply learnings
* **Early reporting prevents escalation and maintains customer trust**

**Slide 8: Support Scenario - Vishing Attack**
* **Caller:** "Hi, this is John from Accounts. I need to verify my account details."
* **Red Flags:** Urgent tone, requesting sensitive info, unfamiliar voice
* **Safe Response:** "I'd be happy to help. Let me call you back on the official number we have on file."
* **Never:** Provide account details, passwords, or process unusual requests

**Slide 9: Customer Support Security Checklist**
- [ ] Always verify before sharing any data
- [ ] Use company-approved tools and channels
- [ ] Never share credentials or OTPs over phone/email
- [ ] Escalate suspicious activity promptly
- [ ] Follow incident reporting procedures
- [ ] Lock workstations when away
- [ ] Mask sensitive data in shared tickets
- [ ] Report phishing attempts immediately
- [ ] Use secure connections for remote support
- [ ] Regular security training participation

**Slide 10: Customer Support Key Takeaways**
* You are the first line of defense for customer trust
* Verification prevents 90% of social engineering attacks
* When in doubt, escalate — don't assume
* Customer data protection is your primary responsibility
* Your vigilance protects both customers and company reputation
