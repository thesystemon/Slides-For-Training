## **PART 4: SOCIAL ENGINEERING DEFENSE (20 Slides)**

**Slide 1: Hook - Human Firewall Can Be Hacked**
* "Hackers don't always break in — sometimes they just ask politely"
* Attackers exploit people, not just systems
* Even with strong tech, social engineering can bypass everything

**Slide 2: What is Social Engineering?**
* **Definition:** Manipulation to reveal information, transfer money, or grant access
* **Method:** Pretending to be trusted entities
* **Core idea:** "Easier to hack a person than a firewall"

**Slide 3: Common Social Engineering Channels**
* **Vishing:** Voice/phone calls
* **Smishing:** SMS/text messages
* **Imposter Calls:** Fake IT, HR, executives
* **Social Media Lures:** Fake job offers, messages
* **In-Person:** Tailgating, impersonation

**Slide 4: Psychological Triggers**
* **Urgency:** "Act in 10 minutes!"
* **Authority:** CEO, police, IT admin
* **Fear:** "Salary won't be processed"
* **Curiosity:** "You've won a gift card"
* **Greed:** "Double your money"
* **Empathy:** "Help me, I need OTP"

**Slide 5: Vishing (Voice Phishing)**
* **Tactic:** Pretending to be trusted entities
* **Common scripts:**
  - "Verify your OTP for security"
  - "I need your login to update systems"
* **Prevention:**
  - Never share OTPs/passwords
  - Hang up and call back officially
  - Verify via internal channels
  - Record and report numbers

**Slide 6: Smishing (SMS Phishing)**
* **Examples:**
  - "Package delivery failed - track here"
  - "Bank account locked - verify now"
* **Red Flags:**
  - Unknown senders
  - Shortened links
  - Misspellings
  - Urgent instructions
* **Safe Action:** Don't click, don't reply, report, delete

**Slide 7: Imposter Calls & CEO Fraud**
* **Scenario:** Urgent call from "CEO" for payment
* **Clues:**
  - Secrecy demands
  - After-hours requests
  - Bypass approval pressure
* **Prevention:**
  - Multi-level approval
  - Secondary verification
  - Official channel confirmation

**Slide 8: Real-World Case Studies**
* **Case 1:** Bank vishing - shared OTP, account drained
* **Case 2:** Fake IT support - remote access granted, CRM compromised
* **Lesson:** Technology can't patch human trust

**Slide 9: Reporting Process**
* Don't engage - hang up immediately
* Note caller ID, time, description
* Report via official channels
* Share screenshots/call logs safely
* Don't forward to colleagues

**Slide 10: Verification Checklist**
* Do I know this person?
* Is the request expected/logical?
* Is this channel official?
* Have I verified through another contact?
* Is there urgency pressure?
* **Any "No" = Stop & Report**

**Slide 11: Organizational Controls**
* Call verification policies
* Security banners for external emails
* Caller ID protection
* Front-desk/finance team training
* VoIP filters and monitoring

**Slide 12: Hands-On Activity 1 - Call Analysis**
* Listen to 3 recorded calls
* Identify real vs fake
* Discuss detection cues
* Share verification strategies

**Slide 13: Hands-On Activity 2 - SMS Quiz**
* Show 5 sample SMS messages
* Identify scams vs legitimate
* Discuss red flags
* Practice safe responses

**Slide 14: Hands-On Activity 3 - Role Play**
* "CEO fraud" scenario simulation
* Test verification procedures
* Practice escalation processes
* Discuss response effectiveness

**Slide 15: Social Media Engineering**
* Fake profiles and connections
* Information gathering
* Fake job offers
* Impersonation attacks
* **Defense:** Verify identities, limit sharing

**Slide 16: Physical Social Engineering**
* Tailgating into secure areas
* Shoulder surfing
* Dumpster diving
* Fake maintenance personnel
* **Defense:** Challenge strangers, wear IDs, secure documents

**Slide 17: Emotional Intelligence Defense**
* Recognize manipulation attempts
* Pause under pressure
* Verify before reacting
* Trust but verify always
* Report suspicious interactions

**Slide 18: Social Engineering KPIs**
* Vishing identification accuracy (≥85%)
* Suspicious call reports trend
* Payment fraud attempts blocked
* Training completion rates
* Incident response times

**Slide 19: Social Engineering Checklist**
- [ ] Verify caller identity officially
- [ ] Never share credentials via phone
- [ ] Question urgent requests
- [ ] Use multi-channel verification
- [ ] Report suspicious communications
- [ ] Follow established procedures
- [ ] Challenge unknown visitors
- [ ] Secure physical access points

**Slide 20: Social Engineering Defense Pledge**
* "I will verify before I trust"
* "I will question unusual requests"
* "I will report suspicious contacts"
* "I will follow established procedures"
* "I will protect through vigilance"
